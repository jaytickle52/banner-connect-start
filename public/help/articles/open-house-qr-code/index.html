Make a QR code for your open house flyer

Banner Connect help · Marketing > QR Codes · Reviewed 2026-11-16

A QR code on your flyer lets a parent sign up for your open house by pointing their phone at it. You make it in Marketing, then QR Codes, and it opens the Event Registration calendar, where families pick the open house date.

Make a QR code for your open house flyer

Create the code in Marketing, then QR Codes, and point it at the Event Registration calendar.

  1. Go to Marketing, then QR Codes, and click Create QR code.
  2. Under QR name, name it for what it does, for example "Open House Sign-up".
  3. Choose Calendar as the type and click Next.
  4. For Resource type, choose Calendars. For Select calendar, choose Event Registration. Click Next.
  5. On the colors and shape step, click Logo to add your school logo (see Put your school logo in the middle of the code).
  6. Click Save.
  7. To print it, open the three-dot menu on the code and choose Download. Use PDF or SVG for anything printed, and PNG for email or social posts.

Before you print, add your open house dates to the Event Registration calendar, so families who scan have a date to pick. See Run an open house with sign-ups and reminders.

How to check it worked: scan the code with your phone's camera. The open house sign-up page opens with your next date.

Put your school logo in the middle of the code

Your logo goes in the middle of the code from the Logo step in Marketing, then QR Codes. Phones still read the code with a small logo in the middle.

  1. While making or editing the code in Marketing, then QR Codes, go to the colors and shape step and click Logo.
  2. Click Browse next to Upload image.
  3. Pick your logo from Media Storage and click Insert media. If it isn't there yet, click Upload to add it first.
  4. Click Save.

A square logo, or just the symbol from your logo, looks best, because a wide logo gets very small in the middle. Don't pick the other company logos shown under "Or use our logos"; they're for businesses linking to those sites.

How to check it worked: the preview on the right shows your logo in the middle, and the code still scans.

Test the QR code before you print

Scan the code from Marketing, then QR Codes, before you send anything to the printer.

  1. Open the code in Marketing, then QR Codes, or download it and open the file.
  2. Point your phone's camera at it and tap the link that appears.
  3. Check that it opens the right page: the open house sign-up, not a visit or a form.
  4. Print one copy at the size you'll use and scan it from about an arm's length away.

Keep the code at least an inch wide on a flyer and bigger on a poster or yard sign, and leave white space around it.

How to check it worked: the right page opens on more than one phone.

Change where the QR code goes after you've printed it

You can change where a code goes in Marketing, then QR Codes, without reprinting it. The flyers already out there start opening the new page.

  1. Go to Marketing, then QR Codes.
  2. Hover the code and click Edit.
  3. Change the calendar, form or link, then click through to Save.

How to check it worked: scan a printed copy. It opens the new page.

Be careful deleting a QR code. Deleting is permanent, and every printed copy stops working. Check with the Banner 61 team first.

How many families scanned our QR code?

Each code counts its scans in Marketing, then QR Codes. Hover the code and click Analytics to see scans over time and by device. Make one code per flyer or per place (one for the newspaper ad, one for the church bulletin) so you can see which one families actually use. Families who sign up after scanning also show up in CRM, then Contacts, like any other sign-up.

Which other QR codes should we make?

Most schools need three codes, all made the same way in Marketing, then QR Codes:

  • Open House Sign-up: type Calendar, then the Event Registration calendar. For flyers, posters and yard signs before an open house.
  • Schedule a Visit: type Calendar, then the Schedule a Visit calendar. For your viewbook, business cards and the welcome sign in your office.
  • Info Packet: type Form, then the Request Info Packet form. For booths, church bulletins and ads, where a parent wants information but isn't ready to visit.

A code that opens a form or calendar adds the family to your account when they finish signing up, so their follow-up starts on its own.
