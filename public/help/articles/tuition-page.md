When a family fills out Request Tuition Info, Banner Connect sends them an email with a link to your tuition information. That link can point to a PDF or to a page on your website. Banner 61 recommends a page.

## Should we send families a tuition page or a PDF?

A page on your website is the better choice for most schools. You change the numbers on one page each year instead of designing, uploading and relinking a new PDF. It fits a phone screen, where most families read your email, while a PDF makes them pinch and zoom. It also works better with screen readers. A PDF is still fine if you prefer it. To swap in next year's PDF, see [Swap the PDF linked in an automatic email](https://demo.banner61.com/help/articles/advanced-and-settings/#swap-the-pdf-linked-in-an-automatic-email).

## What should our tuition page say?

Put tuition and financial aid on one page, with financial aid first. A tuition number on its own can stop a family before they learn they can afford your school, and two separate pages send families looking for the second one.

Start with how your school makes Christian education affordable, then show the numbers:

- How financial aid works at your school and how to apply, with dates
- Scholarships and discounts, such as multi-child, church member, staff and referral
- Payment plan options
- Tuition by grade level
- Fees (application, registration, books, technology) and when each is due
- Who to contact with questions: a name, email and phone number
- One clear next step, such as scheduling a visit or starting an application

To get the page into your email, see [Link your tuition page in the Request Tuition Info email](https://demo.banner61.com/help/articles/tuition-page/#link-your-tuition-page-in-the-request-tuition-info-email).

## Keep your tuition page out of search results and off your menu

Ask whoever manages your website to hide the page from search engines and leave it off the menu, so families reach it only through your email.

1. Ask your website administrator to set the page to "noindex", so search engines don't show it.
2. Ask them to leave it out of the website menu and out of links on other pages.
3. Send the page's address to the Banner 61 team, or add it to the email yourself with [Link your tuition page in the Request Tuition Info email](https://demo.banner61.com/help/articles/tuition-page/#link-your-tuition-page-in-the-request-tuition-info-email).

How to check it worked: search for your school name and "tuition" in a search engine a couple of weeks later. The page shouldn't come up, and it shouldn't appear in your website menu.

Families who asked still see your tuition, with the story behind it, while people comparing schools by price alone don't.

## Link your tuition page in the Request Tuition Info email

Open the Request Tuition Info email in **Marketing**, then **Emails**, then **Templates**, and point its link at your page.

1. Copy your tuition page's address from your browser.
2. Go to **Marketing**, then **Emails**, then the **Templates** tab.
3. Open the **Request Tuition Info (sends on its own)** folder and click the email to open it.
4. Click the words that link to your tuition information, click the link icon in the toolbar, and paste your page's address in place of the old one.
5. Click **Save** at the top right.

How to check it worked: fill out Request Tuition Info on your website with your own email address. The email that arrives should open your tuition page.

Change only the link and wording. The email sends on its own from a workflow, so check with the Banner 61 team before changing anything else about it. Banner 61 can also add the link for you.
